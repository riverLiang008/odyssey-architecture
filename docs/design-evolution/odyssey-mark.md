# How the Odyssey Mark Took Shape

> The Odyssey mark tries to make an abstract idea visible: how a Human-AI Collaboration System can keep changing over time without losing its structure or identity.

Odyssey is not primarily concerned with whether one Prompt can produce an impressive answer. It asks whether an AI Agent capability can move through repeated rounds of configuration, collaboration, revision, and knowledge accumulation until it becomes a system that people can continue to understand and maintain.

The mark therefore could not be only an initial, a robot, or an abstract “AI sparkle.” It needed to express an ongoing movement: beginning with uncertainty, gradually resolving through the combined work of people and Agents, and eventually forming a clear, coherent, and harmonious structure.

## Beginning with a Recursive Spiral

The original visual inspiration came from fractals and recursive spirals.

What interested us about fractals was not simply their apparent complexity, but the way similar relationships can continue across different scales. Local parts change while the whole retains a recognizable order. This resembles Odyssey’s view of long-term evolution: a system may gain new Agents, Workflows, Tools, and Knowledge, but those changes should not erase its responsibility boundaries or collaboration structure.

The earliest concept note in Figma reads: **Evolution without losing identity**. The recursive spiral established the idea the mark needed to carry before it established the final silhouette.

## From Geometric Construction to Sketch Trace

`Geometric Fractal V2` resolves the recursive relationship into more explicit geometric regions and begins removing construction lines so that each region can stand on its own.

A complete geometric construction, however, is not necessarily an effective mark. The mark must remain clear at smaller sizes, and it needs a stable silhouette formed by the relationship between fill and negative space.

`Sketch Trace V3` therefore retains only four filled regions. It preserves the `13 × 8` Fibonacci subdivision but no longer exposes the complete construction process. The fractal changes from an explicit geometric diagram into an order embedded beneath the shape.

V3 establishes the most important foundation for every later version: four regions, a sequence of curves, and spiral movement sustained by negative space.

![Sketch Trace V3: retaining four filled regions and forming the spiral through negative space](/images/design-evolution/odyssey-mark/sketch-trace-v3.png)

*V3 hides the construction lines, leaving four filled regions and the recursive spiral formed by their negative space.*

## Why V3, Not V4

`Inverted Sketch Trace V4` does not redesign the geometry. Instead, it performs a more direct experiment by completely reversing the color and negative space of V3.

The comparison makes the choice clearer. V4 strengthens the outer mass but weakens the sense that the four regions continue along the spiral. The fill direction in V3 makes it easier for the eye to follow the curve inward and leaves room for each region to carry its own meaning.

What survived, then, was not simply “the V3 image,” but the **geometric skeleton, positive-negative relationship, and fill direction** established by V3. The value of V4 is that it tests and clarifies this choice; it is not a failed attempt that needs to be hidden.

![Inverted Sketch Trace V4: reversing color and negative space within the same geometry](/images/design-evolution/odyssey-mark/inverted-trace-v4.png)

*V4 uses the same `13 × 8` geometry as V3 above. Only the positive-negative relationship changes, allowing the two images to show directly why the V3 fill direction was selected.*

## When Thinking Ownership Entered the Mark

With `Semantic Multicolor V5`, the mark begins to use Odyssey’s Thinking Ownership colors. Color no longer serves only to distinguish four regions; it brings the system’s responsibilities and states into the mark:

- **Red** represents Agent-managed open-ended judgment and the uncertainty carried by an Agent;
- **Yellow** represents Shared space, where people and Agents participate and revise together;
- **Green** represents User-managed boundaries that people decide and maintain;
- **Blue** represents the structure and outcomes that gradually settle through the work, while connecting the mark to the overall visual tone of the Odyssey UI.

![Semantic Multicolor V5: introducing Odyssey’s responsibility colors into the V3 geometry](/images/design-evolution/odyssey-mark/semantic-multicolor-v5.png)

*V5 preserves the geometry and positive-negative relationship of V3, replacing only the single gradient with Odyssey’s responsibility colors.*

These colors are not four roles, nor are they a classification system intended to cover every runtime state. They form a visual language: a reminder that different participants contribute to a capability in different ways, and that their work must eventually form a structure that can continue to be used.

V5 therefore marks a crucial change. The mark is no longer only a form inspired by fractals; it begins to carry Odyssey’s own view of systems.

## From V5 to V6: Turning Color into Evolution

`Spiral State Sequence V6` does not overturn the V3 geometry or abandon the V5 colors. It answers one final question: how should these regions be read?

V6 organizes the four regions as a spiral that continues inward. The outer layer retains openness and uncertainty. As the path resolves toward the center, Human-Agent collaboration, human decisions, and established structure no longer appear as isolated color blocks, but as different states within the same process of evolution.

Figma summarizes this direction as: **Uncertain → Resolving → Certain → Odyssey Structure**.

This does not mean that every real task must move linearly through four colors. It expresses a looser direction: uncertainty does not disappear simply because an Agent begins working. It must pass through collaboration, constraint, revision, and consolidation before it can become an outcome that people can understand and maintain.

V6 became the final version because it preserves the continuity of the recursive spiral, the positive-negative relationship of V3, and the Thinking Ownership of V5, while directing all of them toward Odyssey’s central idea: **evolving over time in a clear, coherent, and harmonious way.**

![Spiral State Sequence V6: using four colors to express evolution from uncertainty to Odyssey Structure](/images/design-evolution/odyssey-mark/spiral-state-sequence-v6.png)

*Compared with V5 above, V6 makes the state meaning of the four colors explicit: red is Uncertain, yellow is Resolving, green is Certain, and blue is Odyssey Structure.*

## What a Mark Can Explain

A mark cannot replace product design, nor can it prove that Odyssey has solved every problem in long-term Human-AI collaboration.

It is closer to a compressed design statement: a system may change, but change should leave structure behind; an Agent may carry uncertainty, but autonomy needs boundaries; people and Agents may work together, but that work must eventually settle into outcomes that people can still understand, modify, and continue.

From Recursive Spiral to V6, what changed was not what Odyssey wanted to express, but how that idea gradually acquired clearer geometry, color, and reading order.
